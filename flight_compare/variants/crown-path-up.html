GORT vs FLIGHT — crown-path-up

Experimental variant: crown-path-up — the best option so far plus the upscatter parameter, which acts on the band it does not.

Every gortt curve labelled crown-path-up was produced with -rt_crown_path -rt_upscatter; every curve labelled baseline is Ni et al. (1999) as published, which is what gortt does with no -rt_* option. FLIGHT was not re-run: it is the same Monte Carlo on both pages, so any change here is entirely gortt's. The polar panels show one formulation at a time — the third column, the difference against FLIGHT, is the one to read against the baseline page. The principal-plane and lidar panels show both at once.

How the two models were matched. Crowns are spheroids of semi-axes (r, r, b) with centres uniform in [h1, h2] and uniform in plan at density λ. FLIGHT is given them as an explicit crown list rather than asked to generate its own, and TOTAL_LAI is set negative so it takes the in-crown foliage area density from the per-crown column — the same favd that gortt -favd gets. PROSPECT and the Price soil model are switched off in gortt (-refl_leaf, -trans_leaf, -alb_soil) so both models use identical constants. Before any of this was run, the nadir gap probability was checked between the two models and agrees to better than 1%.
Deliberately not compared: the areal proportions of the scene components (gortt's Kc/Kg/Kt/Kz against FLIGHT's per-component fractions). The lidar waveforms are still compared as normalised shapes, with the energy partition shown separately, because the shape is dP/dz alone and is independent of the backscatter coefficients — but the partition itself is now a real comparison rather than a disclaimer, and it agrees. Overview

What it shows

The overall bias is small; it is the shape of the BRDF that differs. Over all scenes, solar zeniths and view directions, gortt sits at +0.2% bias and 15% RMSD in the red, and −7.4% bias and 19% RMSD in the NIR, relative to the mean FLIGHT BRF.

  • Agreement degrades with view zenith. Red RMSD runs 6.6% within 20° of nadir, 16% at 40–60°, and 27% beyond 60°, where gortt reads high. Its polar plots carry a much stronger bright limb than FLIGHT's, which are comparatively flat.
  • Crown shape is the strongest control. Red RMSD is 7.6% for oblate crowns, 11% for spheres, 24% for prolate. The worst scene is dense-low-prolate, at 34–52% in the red, with the correlation across directions collapsing to −0.25 at a solar zenith of 15°.
  • Red splits by cover (5.1% sparse against 24.5% dense); NIR splits by leaf area (−16% bias at low LAI, unbiased at high LAI), which points at the multiple-scattering treatment rather than the geometry.
  • The forward-scatter half is worse in the NIR (−10.8% bias, 24% RMSD, against −4.6% and 16% in backscatter). The principal-plane panels show gortt's forward limb turning back up into a symmetric U where FLIGHT falls away monotonically — the signature of OPEN-QUESTIONS.md item 9, β in gortt_kc having no view-direction dependence, reached here independently of gortt's own kernel Monte Carlo.
  • The lidar waveform shape is the best-agreeing thing here. With the ground return removed, the canopy profiles differ by an RMSD of 0.009–0.020 on a profile integrating to one, and mean return heights agree to 0.5 m in the high-LAI scenes. gortt's mean return height is biased low, by 1.2–1.4 m in the low-LAI prolate canopies.
  • The lidar energy split agrees, and getting there found a bug in the published model. This comparison originally put gortt's ground fraction at 0.68–0.87 of FLIGHT's — the implied ρg/ρv was exactly 0.426 in gortt, every scene, against 0.79 in FLIGHT. The nadir gap probability had been validated to within 0.8% beforehand, so it could only be the backscatter coefficients, and this page recorded that as an open question for Ni-Meister et al. (2001). It was: the 2018 restatement of that model corrects both coefficients — ρv is built from the leaf reflectance, the hotspot being at scattering angle ξ = π rather than 0, and ρg is the soil albedo outright rather than half of it. With those, gortt's implied ρg/ρv is 0.750 against FLIGHT's 0.791 mean (0.61–0.92 across scenes), and the ground fraction lands within 7% of FLIGHT in every scene (median ratio 0.97, was 0.84). The waveform shapes are byte-unchanged, as they must be.
  • Multiple scattering shows up below the ground, not in the canopy. The orders 1–8 curve is FLIGHT's cumulative waveform over scattering orders 1 to 8, single scattering included. A multiply scattered photon takes a longer route back from the same height, and a longer route is recorded as a lower height, so the ground peak grows a tail downwards: the standard deviation of the sub-1 m return goes from 0.18 m to 0.8–1.8 m, and 1.3–9.2% of the return arrives below −1 m, running to the floor of the recording window at −10 m. Largest in the dense, low-LAI canopies. The canopy shape barely moves, and gortt has no counterpart — Ni-Meister et al. (2001) is single scattering.
  • FLIGHT's ground return sits at −0.198 m, not zero. About −0.15 m of that is real range walk: the source is a point 1 km up, so a photon entering the 25 m footprint off-axis travels further, by E[ρ2]/(2·range) = 0.156 m on average. Shrinking the footprint to 1 m removes it. The remaining −0.05 m is a fixed offset in FLIGHT's range origin, invariant to canopy top, footprint, range, pulse width and bin count. Both are inside one 0.1 m bin and neither affects a shape comparison.

FLIGHT is a reference, not truth. It has its own approximations — leaves as a turbid medium with a disc hotspot correction, an isotropic diffuse sky (g_sky_model is forced to 0 on both branches of flight.c:7409, so the anisotropic lookup table is never read), and Monte Carlo noise of a few tenths of a percent on these averages. The scenes are idealised in both models: no trunks, no understory, Lambertian soil, direct beam only.
